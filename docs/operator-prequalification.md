## Local-content & registration context

*(Expanded from the Parallel local-content run, 2026-06-14. Sources: Namibia MME Final Draft LC Policy Mar 2025; Mozambique DM 55/2024; SA UPRD Act 23 of 2024. These are legal/commercial-structure matters — decisions for Chris and the company's advisors, NOT website claims. See guardrail at the foot.)*

### Namibia (the most material — marine transport is a *named priority sector*)
- **Cabinet-approved Dec 2024; MME Final Draft Mar 2025.** Operators must submit a Local Content Plan as a licence condition and the regulator will run a **local-supplier database** (all contracts advertised locally). Marine transport — *"supply vessels, crew boats"* — is **explicitly a priority local-content category**, so Afrishore's services are directly in scope.
- **The gating test — "local company" = 51% Namibian ownership + 30% PDP (Previously Disadvantaged Persons) equity.** This is a *dual* threshold: meeting 51% Namibian but 0% PDP still fails. Whether an operator can count an Afrishore engagement toward its local-content targets turns on this.
- **Afrishore qualifies in full — confirmed (2026-06-14).** The Namibian entity is **51% Namibian-owned + 30% PDP**, meeting the dual "local company" test completely. Paired with genuine *operational substance* — a real, embedded Walvis Bay presence inside the port's Oil & Gas Section — Afrishore is exactly the compliant local supplier the policy and its anti-fronting enforcement are designed to reward. **Operators can count an Afrishore appointment toward their local-content targets in the priority marine-transport category** — a direct edge over foreign agencies and letterhead-only "locals."
- **No dedicated shipping-agency licence** — operate under Companies Act registration + Directorate of Maritime Affairs (Min. of Works & Transport) oversight + NamRA customs clearing-agent registration. The barrier is the *local-content qualification*, not an agency licence.
- **NAMCOR** procures under the **Public Procurement Act 15 of 2015** (register with the Policy Unit; price preferences for locals; JV weighting where >60% of key employees are Namibian). It maintains restricted bidder lists / EOIs for petroleum suppliers.
- **Watch:** the **Petroleum Amendment Bill 2025** (B.12-2025) moves upstream oversight from the MME to a new **Upstream Petroleum Unit (UPU)** under the Presidency — the local-content enforcement contact point will shift. Monitor passage.
- **Action:** register in the MME local-supplier database when it launches; resolve the ownership-threshold question with advisors.

### Mozambique (reinforces the parked positioning — see [mozambique-positioning] in memory)
- **DM 55/2024 (in force 5 Jul 2024):** concessionaires must ensure **"Economic Substance"** via a Mozambican person — equity, association-in-participation, consortium, *or subcontracting*. National suppliers get a **price preference of up to 10%** above the foreign average. Quarterly REFC reporting to INP.
- **Draft Local Content Law (≈95% drafted, heading to Parliament):** stricter exclusivity regime (≥40% Mozambican ownership / ≥80% national inputs / mostly-national payroll) + a new Local Content Agency. Today's rules are a floor, not a ceiling.
- **No dedicated agency licence** — Ministry of Transport authorisation + local entity (Lda/branch) + NUIT + INSS.
- **Implication for Afrishore:** this *validates* the honest Mozambique framing — real Mozambican work needs a locally-substantive entity or a genuine partner/subcontract relationship (the "Economic Substance" subcontracting route is the lightest-touch entry). Establishing a compliant structure now, under the more flexible DM 55/2024, is a structural advantage before the stricter draft law lands.

### South Africa
- **UPRD Act 23 of 2024:** local-content plans, procurement-progression plans for black-owned suppliers, training-trust fees, and **B-BBEE** as a procurement gatekeeper (PPPFA 80/20 — up to 20 preference points). **Afrishore is B-BBEE Level 1** — maximum procurement recognition (135%), the strongest possible position in SA tenders.

> ✅ **Confirmed credentials (2026-06-14).** Afrishore **is 51% Namibian-owned + 30% PDP in Namibia, and B-BBEE Level 1 in South Africa** — confirmed and current. These are genuine, stateable credentials and strong procurement differentiators: surface them on-site, in listings and in prequalification submissions. Mozambique is the one still to build (Economic Substance via a local entity, consortium or subcontract) if/when formalised. **Still off-limits to claim:** FONASBA / SAAFF / AEO (not held).
